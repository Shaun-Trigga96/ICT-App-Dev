Planning & Scheduling Fundamentals

From L2 Slides
💡
The Big Picture — from your lecturer

Project management = organizing and directing people to achieve a planned result within budget and on schedule. The baseline plan is built at the start; during the project you monitor and intervene when departures occur.

🏗️ Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

The WBS is the hierarchical representation of all tasks in a project. It is the foundation for everything else — schedule, resources, cost estimates.

Think of it like an outline for a research paper: you break the big project into deliverables, then into sub-deliverables, then into individual work packages (the smallest units of manageable work).

📌 Your lecturer's exact definition

A task is the smallest unit of work that is subject to project management. It has a description, a duration, and is assigned to a role. A work package describes the work products to be produced, resources needed, expected duration, dependencies on inputs, acceptance criteria, and responsible person/unit.

📋 Steps to Produce a Project Schedule

  • 1
    Develop the WBS

    List all tasks and their durations. This is the foundation for everything.

  • 2
    Identify Dependencies

    Which tasks must be completed before others can start? Document predecessor relationships.

  • 3
    Estimate Durations

    Use the PERT formula (O + 4M + P) / 6 when uncertain, or historical data for CPM.

  • 4
    Build PERT/CPM Chart

    Draw the network diagram. Assists in assigning tasks and identifying the critical path.

  • 5
    Create Gantt Chart

    Map tasks onto a timeline with bars showing duration and a tracking Gantt for progress.

Critical Path Method (CPM) & Network Diagrams

Exam Heavy
⚠️
Activity on Arrow (AOA) — your lecturer's chosen notation

Your slides specifically say: "We will use 'activity on the arrow'." In this style, arrows = activities and circles (nodes) = milestones/events. Know this distinction for your tests.

🔵 Key Terminology

Milestone / Event
A specific accomplishment that takes place at a particular instance in time. Does NOT consume time or resources. Represented as a circle (node).
Activity
What is done between two milestones. Uses time and resources. Represented as an arrow between two nodes. Cannot start until its preceding event is achieved.
Dummy Activity
Takes no time and no resources. Used to show dependency between events. Drawn as a dashed arrow.
Critical Path
The longest path through the network. Any delay here delays the whole project. All activities on it have Total Float = 0.
EST (Earliest Start Time)
The earliest possible time a milestone can be achieved. Calculated in the forward pass (left to right).
E(j) = max{E(i) + D_ij}
LST (Latest Start Time)
The latest a milestone can be achieved without delaying the project. Calculated in the backward pass (right to left).
L(i) = min{L(j) − D_ij}

⚡ Dummy Activities — Easy to Miss in Tests!

A dummy activity is drawn as a dashed arrow (- - - →). It has zero duration and zero resources. You need them when two activities share the same start AND end node, or when a dependency exists but no real work is involved. Always check your network for these!

🔢 The Algorithms (from your slides — know these!)

Event Numbering Algorithm

Step 1: Give the starting event number 0.

Step 2: Give the next number to any unnumbered event whose predecessor events are each already numbered.

Repeat Step 2 until all events are numbered.

Forward Pass — Earliest Event Time (EST)

Step 1: Set E(0) = 0

Step 2: For j = 1, 2, 3, ..., n:

E(j) = maximum { E(i) + Dij }

where the maximum is over all activities (i,j) that have j as the ending event.

Translation: Take the longest route to reach each node.

Backward Pass — Latest Event Time (LST)

Step 1: Set L(n) = E(n) (the required completion time)

Step 2: For i = n-1, n-2, ..., 0:

L(i) = minimum { L(j) − Dij }

where the minimum is over all activities (i,j) that have i as the starting event.

Translation: Work backwards, taking the shortest route from the end.

📋 5 Steps to Draw & Analyse a CPA Chart

  • 1
    List all project activities and milestones

    Every task, its ID, duration, and what it produces.

  • 2
    Determine dependencies

    What must be finished before each activity can start? Be careful not to add spurious dependencies.

  • 3
    Estimate durations (ED)

    Use CPM (single estimate) or PERT (three estimates). See PERT section.

  • 4
    Draw the network & perform forward/backward passes

    Calculate EST and LST for every node.

  • 5
    Identify the critical path

    Find all nodes where EST = LST (slack = 0). Mark with double bars on the arrows.

⚠️ Common CPM Errors (from your slides)

Your lecturer listed these — watch for them in exercises:

  • ❌ Estimated activity durations may be wrong
  • ❌ Predecessor relationships may contain cycles
  • ❌ List of prerequisites contains more than immediate predecessors
  • ❌ Some predecessor relationships may be overlooked
  • ❌ Some listed predecessor relationships may be spurious (fake)
  • ❌ Some tasks may be missing entirely

PERT — Program Evaluation & Review Technique

Core Formulas
🔑
CPM vs PERT — The Key Difference (from your slides)

CPM = deterministic durations (you know exactly how long). Uses data from previous similar projects. Suited to construction-type projects.
PERT = probabilistic durations (you're uncertain). Better for Research & Development projects with limited historical data. PERT = CPM + probabilistic task times.

📊 The Three PERT Time Estimates

SymbolNameDefinition (from your slides)
A (or O) Optimistic Time Minimum time if everything goes right. Assumed NOT to be delayed by even likely events like employee absence.
M Most Likely Time Duration under normal working conditions. The most frequent duration based on past experience.
B (or P) Pessimistic Time Time required under particularly bad circumstances. Equipment arrives late, technical problems occur, staff ill. Should be exceeded no more than 1% of the time.
📌

These three estimates assume a Beta distribution for each task duration — this is why the formula weights M four times more heavily than A or B.

⭐ The PERT Expected Duration Formula

Te = (A + 4M + B) / 6
Where A = Optimistic, M = Most Likely, B = Pessimistic
M is weighted 4 times because it is the most likely outcome

Variance of One Activity

σ² = ((B − A) / 6)²

Standard Deviation

σ = (B − A) / 6

Project Variance (Critical Path)

σ²_project = Σσ²_critical

Z-Score for Probability

z = (T_target − T_e) / σ_project
📌
Important: The 50% Rule

From your PERT slides: "because the durations are probabilistic, when we find the duration for a project, it means there is a 50% chance of finishing the project by that time." This is why we use the Z-score formula to find the probability of meeting a specific target date.

🎯 Using the Z-Score for Probability of Meeting a Deadline

  • 1
    Calculate Te for all activities

    Use Te = (A + 4M + B) / 6 for every activity on the critical path.

  • 2
    Sum Te for the critical path

    This gives you the expected project completion time (Te_project). Remember: 50% probability of finishing by this date.

  • 3
    Calculate σ² for each critical path activity

    Use σ² = ((B − A) / 6)²

  • 4
    Sum all critical path variances

    σ²_project = Σσ²_i (for all i on critical path only)

  • 5
    Find σ_project = √(σ²_project)

    Take the square root to get the standard deviation of the project.

  • 6
    Calculate Z

    z = (T_target − Te_project) / σ_project

  • 7
    Look up Z in standard normal table

    Or use Excel: =NORMSDIST(z) to find the probability.

🎲 Step 5 — Probability Calculation

What is the probability of finishing within 15 days?

Critical path variances only (activities 1→3, 3→4, 4→5):

σ²_project = 1.78 + 0.11 + 2.78 = 4.67

σ_project = √4.67 = 2.16 days

Z = (T_target − Te) / σ = (15 − 13) / 2.16 = +0.93

Types of Float (Directly from Your Slides)

Often Tested
📌
Your lecturer defined THREE types of float

Most textbooks only cover Total Float — but your slides also define Free Float and Independent Float. Make sure you know all three!

Total Float
The maximum delay that can be assigned to any activity without delaying the entire project, assuming no delays in other activities.
TF = L(j) − E(i) − D_ij
Critical activity = TF of 0
Free Float
The delay that can be assigned to an activity without delaying subsequent activities — takes into account the effect on activities that come afterwards.
FF = E(j) − E(i) − D_ij
Independent Float
Delay possible without affecting subsequent OR preceding activities. Calculated assuming worst case: predecessors finish at their latest times, successors begin at their earliest times.
IF = E(j) − L(i) − D_ij
If negative → treat as 0

💰 Cost/Time Trade-Off — Crashing (from your slides)

Your slides note: "Most activities can be done faster if you pay more money — work overtime, hire more people, rent extra equipment, incentive contracts."

PERT/CPM has the ability to crash a schedule — shorten activity time to reduce overall project completion time, at the cost of increased spending. You crash activities on the critical path only — there's no benefit to crashing non-critical activities.

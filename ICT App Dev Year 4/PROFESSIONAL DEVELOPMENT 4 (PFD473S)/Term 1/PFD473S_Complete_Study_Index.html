Core Formulas
Every formula you need
Learn these — they appear in every PERT question.
PERT Expected Duration (Te)
Te = (O + 4M + P) / 6
O = Optimistic  |  M = Most Likely  |  P = Pessimistic  |  M is weighted 4× because it is the most likely outcome
Variance (one activity)
σ² = ((P − O) / 6)²
Project variance
σ²_proj = Σ σ²_critical
Project std deviation
σ_proj = √σ²_proj
Z-score (probability)
Z = (T_target − Te) / σ
Target date for X% confidence
T = Te + Z × σ
Common Z values
90%→1.28   95%→1.645
Total Float (TF)
L(j) − E(i) − Duration
Free Float (FF)
E(j) − E(i) − Duration
Independent Float (IF)
max{0, E(j) − L(i) − D}
Forward Pass
E(j) = max{ E(i) + D }
Backward Pass
L(i) = min{ L(j) − D }
Critical Path Rule
TF = 0 on every activity
The project Te gives you a 50% probability of finishing on time. You need the Z-score formula for any other probability target.
How-to
PERT — step by step
Follow this order for every PERT question in the exam.
The 5-step PERT process
1
Calculate Te and σ² for every activity

Use Te = (O + 4M + P) / 6 and σ² = ((P − O) / 6)² for every row in the table. Write the working clearly — marks are given for the calculation, not just the answer.

2
Draw the AOA network and run the Forward Pass

Arrows = activities (use Te values). Circles = nodes. Dashed arrows = dummy activities (0 duration). Forward pass: E(j) = max{ E(i) + Te }. Take the longest route into each node.

3
Run the Backward Pass and find Total Float

Start at the end: set L(end) = E(end). Work backwards: L(i) = min{ L(j) − Te }. Then for each activity: TF = L(j) − E(i) − Te. Activities with TF = 0 are critical.

4
State the Critical Path and expected project duration

List the critical path as a sequence: e.g. A → B → D → F → H. The project duration = sum of Te on the critical path. State: "There is a 50% probability of finishing by this date."

5
Probability calculation (if asked)

Sum σ² for critical path activities only → take √ to get σ_project → apply Z = (T − Te) / σ for probability, or T = Te + Z × σ for a confidence date.

Float
Three types of float
Exam Q1.3 (2024) asked for all three for non-critical activities.
■  Total Float (TF)
L(j) − E(i) − Duration

How much an activity can be delayed without delaying the whole project. Uses Latest possible times.

■  Free Float (FF)
E(j) − E(i) − Duration

How much an activity can be delayed without delaying the earliest start of its successor. Uses Earliest times.

■  Independent Float (IF)
max{0, E(j) − L(i) − Duration}

Delay possible with no effect on anything — predecessors finish as late as possible, successors start as early as possible. Never goes negative (use 0).

Management Theory
Theory answers
Both papers include a 20-mark theory section. Definitions and applications.
Project Management — Definition (2024 Exam Q3.1, 4 marks)

Project Management is the application of knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to project activities in order to meet project requirements. (PMBOK Guide)


It is accomplished through the application and integration of five process groups: Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring & Controlling, and Closing.


Alternatively (functional view): PM is the process of guiding a project from beginning to end using management models — encompassing Planning, Organizing, Controlling, and Terminating.

The Iron Triangle — Triple Constraint (2024 Exam Q3.2, 4 marks)

The fundamental trade-off is known as the Triple Constraint or Iron Triangle. Every project is simultaneously constrained by:


Scope — what work needs to be done; features and deliverables.

Time — the schedule; when the project must be completed.

Cost — the budget; how much money is available.

Quality is often added as a fourth central constraint inside the triangle.


The trade-off: changing any one constraint forces a change in at least one of the others. For example, if you shorten the timeline you must either reduce scope OR increase cost.

Fayol's Principles — 4 for exam (2024 Q3.3, 12 marks)

Each principle = 3 marks: name + definition + application. Below are well-tested choices.

1. Specialisation of Labour (Division of Work)
Specialising encourages continuous improvement in skills. In an ICT project: developers develop, testers test, PM plans — not everyone doing everything. Increases quality and speed.

4. Unity of Command
Each employee has one and only one boss. In project management this is violated in a matrix organisation, where a team member reports to both a department head and a project manager. Fayol considered this a major disadvantage of the matrix structure.

14. Esprit de Corps
Harmony and cohesion among personnel. Management should foster teamwork and mutual trust. Fayol warned against "divide and rule" strategies, which destroy team spirit.

Other valid choices: Authority (#2), Discipline (#3), Unity of Direction (#5), Remuneration (#7), Scalar Chain (#9), Equity (#11), Initiative (#13)

Mintzberg's 10 Management Roles (Test 1 Q3.2, 10 marks)

Mintzberg identified 10 roles in 3 categories. He argued they are inseparable — the whole is greater than the sum of its parts.

CategoryRoleWhat the manager does
InterpersonalFigureheadPerforms ceremonial duties as head of unit — attending functions, meeting dignitaries.
LeaderResponsible for the work of people in their unit. Motivates and guides. PMs often rely on influence, not formal authority.
LiaisonMakes contacts outside the vertical chain of command. Builds informal external information networks.
InformationalMonitorScans environment for information — including gossip, hearsay, and speculation ("soft information").
DisseminatorShares information with team members. Managers who hoard information fail their teams.
SpokespersonPasses information outside the unit — presenting project status to senior managers or clients.
DecisionalEntrepreneurInitiates voluntary change — identifies opportunities and drives improvements.
Disturbance HandlerResponds to involuntary situations and crises — strikes, loss of key staff, failing vendor.
Resource AllocatorDecides who gets what — time, money, equipment, personnel. Determines project priorities.
NegotiatorNegotiates with parties inside and outside the organisation — contracts, deadlines, resources.

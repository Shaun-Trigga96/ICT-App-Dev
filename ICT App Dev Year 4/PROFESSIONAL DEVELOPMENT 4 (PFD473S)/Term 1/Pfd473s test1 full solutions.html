Question 3 — Management Theory

[20 Marks]

3.1 — Name and describe any THREE of Henri Fayol's principles of management

[10 marks]
Fayol described 14 principles. You only need THREE — but give enough detail to earn all marks. Each principle should include: the name, a definition, and a brief explanation. Below are well-chosen examples.
Principle 4: Unity of Command
Definition: Each employee has one and only one boss.

Explanation: Every worker should receive orders from a single supervisor only. Having two or more managers giving instructions to the same person causes confusion, divided loyalty, and conflicting priorities. This principle is particularly relevant in project management — it is specifically violated in a matrix organisation, where a team member reports to both a department head and a project manager simultaneously. Fayol considered this violation a major disadvantage of the matrix structure.
Principle 1: Specialisation of Labour (Division of Work)
Definition: Specialising encourages continuous improvement in skills and the development of improved methods.

Explanation: When individuals focus on specific tasks they become more efficient and skilled over time. Rather than one person performing all tasks, work should be divided according to expertise. In an ICT project, for example, developers should develop, testers should test, and project managers should plan — rather than everyone doing everything. This increases quality and productivity.
Principle 14: Esprit de Corps
Definition: Harmony and cohesion among personnel.

Explanation: Management should foster teamwork, mutual trust, and a sense of collective identity among employees. A united team performs better than a collection of individuals working in isolation. In a software development project, encouraging team members to support each other, celebrate milestones together, and communicate openly builds morale and improves outcomes. Fayol warned against "divide and rule" strategies, which destroy esprit de corps.
Other valid choices you could use: Authority (#2), Discipline (#3), Unity of Direction (#5), Remuneration (#7), Scalar Chain (#9), Equity (#11), Initiative (#13)

3.2 — List and briefly explain the roles of managers as described by Henry Mintzberg

[10 marks]
Mintzberg identified 10 roles grouped into 3 categories. He argued these roles are inseparable — they form a gestalt (the integrated whole is greater than the sum of its parts). He also noted that "surprisingly little study" had been done on what managers actually do.
RoleCategoryExplanation
INTERPERSONAL ROLES (arise from the manager's formal authority)
FigureheadInterpersonal Performs ceremonial duties by virtue of their position as head of a unit — e.g. attending employee functions, meeting dignitaries, hosting important clients. Applies to project managers too.
LeaderInterpersonal Responsible for the work of the people in their unit. Motivates, encourages, and guides team members. PMs often have high responsibility but little formal authority, so they rely heavily on influence and leadership.
LiaisonInterpersonal Makes contacts outside the vertical chain of command (boundary-crossing). Builds informal external information networks. Interfacing with people outside the project team is critical for gathering information and resources.
INFORMATIONAL ROLES (arise from the manager's information access — approx. 40% of time)
MonitorInformational Constantly scans the environment for information, including gossip, hearsay, and speculation ("soft information"). This helps the manager detect problems before they become serious.
DisseminatorInformational Shares and distributes information to team members. Managers who hoard information (treating it as power) fail their teams — members cannot make effective decisions without it.
SpokespersonInformational Passes information to people outside their unit — presenting project status to senior managers, clients, or stakeholders.
DECISIONAL ROLES (arise from the manager's authority and information)
EntrepreneurDecisional Proactively initiates change and development projects based on opportunities identified during monitoring. The manager acts as an innovator. This role is initiated by the manager.
Disturbance HandlerDecisional Reacts to pressures where change is outside the manager's control — scope changes, conflicts, accidents, loss of key personnel. This role is reactive, unlike the Entrepreneur role.
Resource AllocatorDecisional Decides who gets what resources — time, money, equipment, people. One of the most important resources the manager allocates is their own time. Access to the manager signals priority.
NegotiatorDecisional Negotiates with stakeholders, vendors, and other parties on behalf of the organisation or project. Linked closely to the Resource Allocator role — you need authority over resources to negotiate effectively.

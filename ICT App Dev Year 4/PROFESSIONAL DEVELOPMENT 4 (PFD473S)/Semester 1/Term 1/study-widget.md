## SO1 — Introduction to Project Management

### What is a project? (PMBOK definition)

> "A project is a **temporary endeavour** undertaken to create a **unique product, service, or result**."

Projects and operations differ in that **operations are ongoing and repetitive**, while **projects are temporary and unique**.

| Characteristic | Description |
|---|---|
| **Temporary** | Every project has a definite beginning and end. End = objectives achieved OR it's clear they can't be met. Temporary does NOT mean short. |
| **Unique** | Creates unique deliverables: a product, a capability to perform a service, or a result (e.g. a research finding). |
| **Progressive elaboration** | Developing in steps, continuing by increments. Scope is broad early, becomes more explicit over time. **NOT the same as scope creep.** |
| **Shared with operations** | Both are: performed by people · constrained by limited resources · planned, executed and controlled. |

---

### The Iron Triangle (Project Constraints)

There is a fundamental trade-off between three constraints — changing one affects the others:

```
           Scope
            /\
           /  \
          /    \
         / (Q)  \
        /________\
      Cost       Time
```

**(Q) = Quality** sits at the centre as the fourth constraint.

---

### Project Management definition

> "The application of knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to project activities to meet project requirements." — PMBOK

Accomplished through: **Initiating → Planning → Executing → Monitoring & Controlling → Closing**

---

### The 4 PM Activities (Functional view)

| Activity | Description |
|---|---|
| **Planning** | Specifying results, activities, schedule, and resources needed. |
| **Organizing** | Defining the project structure, roles and responsibilities. Mapping roles to work. |
| **Controlling** | Identifying deviations from plan, reallocating resources, changing schedules when needed. |
| **Terminating** | Delivering to the client, reviewing lessons learned, modifying templates for future projects. |

---

### The PM's Role

The project manager is responsible for:

- Identifying requirements
- Establishing clear and achievable objectives
- Balancing the competing demands for quality, scope, time and cost
- Adapting plans to the concerns of various stakeholders
- PMs often have **high responsibility but little formal authority** — they must use **influence** to get things done

---

### Organisation Types — know all three

**Functional (Line) Organisation**
- People grouped by speciality into departments
- Projects pass through a pipeline from department to department
- Fixed, permanent structure
- Few participants involved for the project's full duration

**Project-Based Organisation**
- Participants assigned to one project at a time for its entire duration
- Dynamically changing set of projects — created on request, terminated on delivery
- No projects are permanently part of the organisation

**Matrix Organisation**
- Combines functional and project-based
- People work in both a department AND on projects simultaneously
- Each person reports to **TWO people**: the department head AND the project manager
- **Violates Fayol's "Unity of command"** — this is a major disadvantage
- Allows rapid response to project needs and easy recruitment of specialised skills

### Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

- The **hierarchical representation of all tasks** in a project
- Foundation for everything: schedule, resources, cost estimates
- A **task** = the smallest unit of work subject to project management
- A **work package** describes: work products to be produced, resources needed, expected duration, dependencies, acceptance criteria, and responsible person
- Any work product delivered to the customer = a **deliverable**
- Work products consumed only by project participants = **internal work products**

## SO2 — Henri Fayol

### Fayol's 5 Management Functions

| Function | Description |
|---|---|
| **Prevoyant (Forecast & Plan)** | Examine the future and draw up a plan of action. The elements of strategy. |
| **Organise** | Build up the material and human structure of the undertaking. |
| **Command** | Maintain the activity among personnel. |
| **Coordinate** | Binding together, unifying and harmonising all activity and effort. |
| **Control** | Seeing that everything occurs in conformity with established rules and expressed commands. |

---

### Fayol's 14 Management Principles

| # | Principle | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Specialisation of labour** | Encourages continuous improvement in skills and development of better methods. |
| 2 | **Authority** | The right to give orders and the power to exact obedience. |
| 3 | **Discipline** | No slacking, no bending of rules. |
| 4 | **Unity of command** | Each employee has **one and only one boss**. Matrix orgs violate this! |
| 5 | **Unity of direction** | A single mind generates a single plan; all play their part in it. |
| 6 | **Subordination of individual interests** | At work, only work things should be pursued or thought about. |
| 7 | **Remuneration** | Employees receive fair payment for services — not what the company can get away with. |
| 8 | **Centralisation** | Consolidation of management functions; decisions made from the top. |
| 9 | **Scalar chain (line of authority)** | Formal chain of command running from top to bottom, like military. |
| 10 | **Order** | All materials and personnel have a prescribed place and must remain there. |
| 11 | **Equity** | Equality of treatment (but not necessarily identical treatment). |
| 12 | **Personnel tenure** | Limited turnover of personnel; lifetime employment for good workers. |
| 13 | **Initiative** | Thinking out a plan and doing what it takes to make it happen. |
| 14 | **Esprit de corps** | Harmony and cohesion among personnel. |

> **Exam trap:** Don't confuse Remuneration (fair pay) with Equity (equal treatment). They are different principles.

## SO2 — Henry Mintzberg

### Key insight

Mintzberg argued there had been "surprisingly little study of what managers actually do." He identified **10 roles** in **3 categories**. The roles are **inseparable** — they form a gestalt (the integrated whole is greater than the sum of its parts).

---

### The 3 Categories and 10 Roles

#### Interpersonal Roles

| Role | Description |
|---|---|
| **Figurehead** | Performs ceremonial duties by virtue of position (lunching with customers, attending employee weddings, meeting dignitaries). Some appear trivial but are important to smooth functioning. Applies to project managers too. |
| **Leader** | Responsible for the work of people in the unit. Motivates and encourages the team. In a project org = direct role; in a matrix = indirect role. PMs often have high responsibility but little formal authority, so must rely on influence. |
| **Liaison** | Makes contacts **outside** the vertical chain of command (boundary-crossing role). Builds up external informal information systems. Interfacing with people outside the unit is critical to project team success. |

#### Informational Roles (~40% of manager's time)

| Role | Description |
|---|---|
| **Monitor** | Constantly scans the environment for information. Much is oral — gossip, hearsay, speculation. This "soft information" alerts the manager to problems before they occur. |
| **Disseminator** | Distributes information to the team. Managers who hoard information (treating it as power) fail here — team members can't make effective decisions without it. |
| **Spokesperson** | Passes information to people **outside** their unit — presentations to higher managers, clients, or stakeholders concerned about a major project. |

#### Decisional Roles

| Role | Description |
|---|---|
| **Entrepreneur** | Initiates development projects when a good idea is found during monitoring. Technical PMs often suggest new product development projects to senior management. |
| **Disturbance handler** | **Reacts** to pressures where change is **outside** their control — scope changes, accidents, loss of key personnel, conflicts with functional managers over priorities. |
| **Resource allocator** | Decides who gets what resources — including the manager's own time. Access to the manager exposes people to the nerve centre of the project. |
| **Negotiator** | Negotiates with stakeholders, vendors, and other parties. Needs authority over resources to negotiate meaningfully. |

> **Exam trap:** Entrepreneur = **initiated** by the manager (found an opportunity). Disturbance Handler = **reactive** (external pressure forces a response).

## Self-Test Questions

Test yourself — try to answer before reading the explanation.

---

**Q1.** Which of the following best defines a project?

- A) Ongoing, repetitive work done by a department
- B) A temporary endeavour to create a unique product, service, or result ✅
- C) A plan that describes activities and their durations
- D) A budget allocated to a team for operational work

*The PMBOK definition: "a temporary endeavour undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result." Operations are ongoing and repetitive — the opposite of a project.*

---

**Q2.** A matrix organisation violates which of Fayol's 14 principles?

- A) Esprit de corps
- B) Centralisation
- C) Unity of command ✅
- D) Scalar chain

*In a matrix org, each member reports to BOTH a department head AND a project manager — two bosses. This directly violates "Unity of command."*

---

**Q3.** In Mintzberg's model, the Disseminator role falls under which category?

- A) Interpersonal
- B) Informational ✅
- C) Decisional
- D) Operational

*Informational roles are Monitor, Disseminator, and Spokesperson — roughly 40% of a manager's time.*

---

**Q4.** What does "progressive elaboration" mean in project management?

- A) Scope creep — adding features without approval
- B) Developing the project in steps, with scope becoming more detailed over time ✅
- C) The process of closing the project progressively
- D) Estimating costs progressively as the team grows

*Progressive elaboration = developing in steps. Scope is described broadly early, then made more explicit as understanding grows. Your slides specifically note: NOT the same as scope creep.*

---

**Q5.** Which Fayol principle states that employees should receive fair payment?

- A) Equity
- B) Authority
- C) Remuneration ✅
- D) Personnel tenure

*Remuneration: "employees receive fair payment for services, not what the company can get away with." Equity is about equality of treatment — a different principle entirely.*

---

**Q6.** The Disturbance Handler role is triggered by what?

- A) The manager finds a new business opportunity
- B) The manager reacts to pressures where change is outside their control ✅
- C) The manager allocates the project budget
- D) The manager represents the team to outsiders

*Disturbance Handler = reactive. Examples: scope changes, accidents, loss of key personnel, conflicts with functional managers. Compare to Entrepreneur, which is initiated by the manager.*

---

**Q7.** Which organisation type assigns participants to ONE project at a time for its full duration?

- A) Functional organisation
- B) Matrix organisation
- C) Project-based organisation ✅
- D) Geographic organisation

*Project-based org: participants are assigned to only one project at a time for the project's entire duration. It consists of a dynamically changing set of projects.*

## Quick Reference — Key Distinctions for the Test

| Concept | Remember this |
|---|---|
| Project vs Operations | Project = temporary & unique. Operations = ongoing & repetitive. |
| Progressive elaboration vs Scope creep | Elaboration = planned, incremental detail. Scope creep = uncontrolled additions. |
| Matrix org violation | Violates Fayol's Unity of Command (#4) — two bosses. |
| Entrepreneur vs Disturbance handler | Entrepreneur = manager initiates. Disturbance = manager reacts. |
| Monitor vs Disseminator | Monitor = receives/gathers info. Disseminator = shares info with the team. |
| Remuneration vs Equity | Remuneration = fair pay. Equity = equal treatment. |
| Informational roles % | ~40% of a manager's time. |

Research: A Way of Thinking

Semester 1, Week 1 · Kumar Chapter 1
In-class · pairs

Topic Triage — Researchable vs Too Broad

Work in pairs and complete each step.

What makes a justification strong?

It names the context, group and focus, shows the question can be answered with real evidence, and confirms it is feasible in the semester with the access you have. Weekly artefact to submit: three topic options with a short feasibility justification.

Now you try

Narrowing broad ideas into researchable topics

Model direction

e.g. "How does the use of a specific LMS quiz feature affect test scores among first-year ICT students over one semester?" and "What types of generative-AI study tools do second-year ICT students use, and for which tasks?" — both name a group, setting and measurable focus.

Higher-order · reflection

Analysis & application prompts

  1. A lecturer claims "students are cheating more because of AI." Break the claim into parts and propose what evidence you would need to evaluate it.
  2. Compare two explanations for low engagement in an online coding platform. How would you design a small study to test which is more plausible?
  3. An ICT department wants a new ticketing system. What would controlled and empirical look like in a practical evaluation?
  4. You have helpdesk logs and satisfaction surveys. How would being systematic and critical guide combining and interpreting them?
  5. A student proposes "I want to study blockchain." Why is this too broad? Rewrite it into a researchable, feasible topic.

The Research Process: A Quick Glance

Semester 1, Week 2 · Kumar Chapter 2
Class exercise

Map your project to the research process

Exit ticket

Connect data collection to an objective

Write a short paragraph explaining how your proposed data collection will answer one of your objectives.

What 'alignment' looks like

If the objective is to measure frequency of tool use, the data should be a survey with usage-frequency options — not open interviews. If the objective is to explore experiences, use interviews — not CPU logs. The instrument must produce evidence that directly answers the objective.

Higher-order · reflection

Analysis prompts

  1. How does a weakly defined research problem create problems later in sampling, data collection and analysis? Use an ICT example.
  2. A student wants to study "AI in education." How would you guide them to a specific problem and two aligned objectives?
  3. Compare a survey design and a case study for investigating student experiences of an online assessment platform — what conclusions can each support?
  4. How does the golden thread help you decide which questions to include or exclude in a questionnaire?
  5. A project collected detailed interview data but wrote objectives requiring numerical measurement. What is the mismatch, and how could it be corrected?
Topic 3

Research Ethics

Special Lecture (Francke, Hendricks & Kabaso, 16 Feb 2026) + 'The ethics of using generative AI in research'
Reflection

Apply the principles to your study

For your proposed study, note how you would satisfy each principle.

Case analysis

What went wrong?

Pick one case from the notes (Briggs, Murdoch & Barwood, Zegeye, Robinson, or citation cartels). Which principle(s) were breached, and what would ethical conduct have looked like?

Hint

Most cases breach Integrity (fabrication/plagiarism) and Transparency. Citation cartels also corrupt authorship and the integrity of the scholarly record. Ethical conduct = real data, honest reporting, accurate authorship, and full disclosure.

AI ethics debate

Deontological vs teleological

The lecture used an art analogy: is identical, pixel-by-pixel artwork less deserving of a prize if made with AI? Decide where you stand for research: should we judge the process (deontological) or the outcome (teleological)?

The lecture's stance

For research and science, a teleological assessment should take priority — AI should be used where it is a useful tool for advancing knowledge, provided the four conditions (human responsibility, transparency, integrity/accuracy, originality/authorship) are met.

Topic 8

Constructing the Research Aim & Sub-Objectives

Semester 1, Week 8 · Kumar Ch.4 & Creswell Ch.7
Practical · this week

Build your aim, questions & objectives

Action verbs to choose from

To describe, identify, determine, measure, assess, compare, examine, explore, analyse, evaluate, establish, test. Pick the verb that matches your intended design: describe→descriptive, examine the relationship/test→correlational or experimental, compare→comparison, explore→qualitative.

Align

Map objectives to questions (one-to-one)

Take three of your objectives and write the single research question that matches each. Check they share the same focus and specificity.

Self-check

If a question introduces a focus your objective never mentioned (or vice-versa), the thread is broken — realign the wording. Every objective should be answerable through data collection and analysis.

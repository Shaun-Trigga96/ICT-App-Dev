🌳 COMPARISON TREE · when to use (and when to avoid)
📊 RESEARCH APPROACHES
📈 Quantitative
✅ WHEN TO USE: measuring variables, testing hypotheses, large samples, statistical generalisation, cause-effect, surveys with closed items, experiments.
❌ AVOID WHEN: you need deep lived experiences, exploring new phenomena, unknown variables, or when context is more important than numbers.
🗣️ Qualitative
✅ WHEN TO USE: exploring meanings, perceptions, processes (how/why), small purposive samples, interviews, observations, thematic analysis.
❌ AVOID WHEN: you need to generalise to a population, test statistical relationships, or measure precise effect sizes.
💡 Mixed methods: use both when one type cannot fully answer the research question (e.g., survey + follow-up interviews).
🎲 SAMPLING STRATEGIES
🎯 Probability (Random)
✅ WHEN TO USE: you need representative samples, generalisation to population, inferential statistics, reduce selection bias, large sample frame available.
❌ AVOID WHEN: population list unavailable, resources limited, or research question does not require generalisation.
🧩 Non-probability
✅ WHEN TO USE: exploratory/qualitative studies, hard-to-reach populations, purposive selection of information-rich cases, convenience for pilot studies.
❌ AVOID WHEN: you need to make strong statistical generalisations or when selection bias would invalidate conclusions.
📌 Common non-probability: purposive, snowball, quota, convenience. Probability: simple random, stratified, cluster.
📚 LITERATURE REVIEW APPROACHES
📑 PRISMA / Systematic Review
✅ WHEN TO USE: answer a focused, answerable question; need transparent, repeatable search; synthesise all available evidence; minimise bias; meta‑analysis possible.
❌ AVOID WHEN: topic too broad, research question exploratory, or you have very few existing studies. Requires time & methodological discipline.
📖 Narrative / Traditional Review
✅ WHEN TO USE: establishing background, developing theoretical framework, describing historical trends, introducing a broad topic, or when a systematic review is not feasible.
❌ AVOID WHEN: you claim to be “systematic” without a protocol, or risk cherry-picking evidence — then the Golden Thread weakens.
🔍 PRISMA = explicit inclusion/exclusion + flow diagram. Narrative = expert synthesis, but less reproducible.
📉 DATA ANALYSIS LEVELS
📊 Descriptive statistics
✅ WHEN TO USE: summarise sample characteristics, frequencies, means, percentages; show patterns without generalising to population; any study with quantitative data.
❌ AVOID AS FINAL STEP: if your research question requires testing hypotheses or making inferences about a wider population.
📈 Inferential statistics
✅ WHEN TO USE: test relationships, differences, predictions; generalise from sample to population; require probability sampling and sufficient sample size.
❌ AVOID WHEN: your sample is non‑random, too small, or research question is purely descriptive/exploratory.
⚙️ e.g., t‑test, ANOVA, chi‑square, regression → require assumptions. Descriptive = mean, SD, frequencies.
⏱️ TIME HORIZON
📸 Cross‑sectional
✅ WHEN TO USE: snapshot at one point, prevalence studies, descriptive relationships, limited time/resources, attitudes and perceptions now.
❌ AVOID WHEN: you need to study change over time, causal inference (without special techniques), or trends.
📆 Longitudinal
✅ WHEN TO USE: track changes, developmental trends, before‑after interventions, panel studies, causal sequences. Strong for evaluating ICT training or adoption over time.
❌ AVOID WHEN: time, budget, attrition risk high, or research question is a single snapshot.
📌 Longitudinal subtypes: trend, cohort, panel. Cross‑sectional is more common in student projects.
🧭 RESEARCH PURPOSE
🧪 Exploratory
✅ WHEN TO USE: new or under‑researched area, generate hypotheses, understand “what is happening”, flexible design, qualitative or mixed methods.
❌ AVOID WHEN: you already have strong theory and need to test precise predictions.
⚖️ Confirmatory
✅ WHEN TO USE: test existing theory/hypotheses, predetermined variables, quantitative design, experiments, structured surveys.
❌ AVOID WHEN: you have little prior knowledge, or the concepts are not yet clearly defined.
🔁 Exploratory often leads to confirmatory studies later — Golden Thread still applies.
🗂️ DATA SOURCES
📝 Primary data
✅ WHEN TO USE: specific questions not answered by existing data; you control measurement; need fresh insights (surveys, interviews, experiments).
❌ AVOID WHEN: high cost, time constraints, access to participants problematic, or reliable secondary data already exists.
💾 Secondary data
✅ WHEN TO USE: large-scale datasets, logs, archives, reuse for new questions, cost/time efficient (e.g., LMS logs, GitHub data, government stats).
❌ AVOID WHEN: data does not fit your variables, lacks quality, or ethical permission missing.
⚡ ICT secondary examples: server logs, ticketing systems, commit history, API usage metrics — check completeness.
